\section{A General Nonviscous Damping Formulation}
Consider the general equation of motion of a dynamic system under external load $\bp\left(t\right)$,
\begin{gather}
    \br\left(\bu,\bv,\ba\right)=\bp\left(t\right),
\end{gather}
where $\br=\br\left(\bu,\bv,\ba\right)$ is the overall resistance of the system, accounting for contributions by displacement $\bu$, velocity $\bv$ and acceleration $\ba$.
Additional energy dissipating term can be introduced, for example,
\begin{gather}\label{eq:general_eom}
    \br+\bbf=\bp,
\end{gather}
in which $\bbf$ can be additively decomposed such that
\begin{gather}
    \bbf=\sum\bbf_j.
\end{gather}
Each term $\bbf_j$ is assumed to be governed by a first-order differential equation such that
\begin{gather}\label{eq:general_de}
    \dot{\bbf}_j=g\left(\bbf_j,\by_j\right)
\end{gather}
where $\by_j=\by_j\left(\bu,\bv,\ba\right)$ is an internal field that depends on $\bu$, $\bv$ and/or $\ba$.
Given that $\bv=\dot{\bu}$ and $\ba=\ddot{\bu}$ depend on $\bu$, eventually $\by_j=\by_j\left(\bu\right)$.
For simplicity, we assume each term $\bbf_j$ is governed by the following differential equation:
\begin{gather}
    \dot{\bbf}_j=-s_j\bbf_j+m_j\by_j
\end{gather}
where $s_j\in\mathbb{R}$ and $m_j\in\mathbb{R}$ are two real scalar parameters that control the response (and can be extended to the complex realm $\mathbb{C}$ subject to additional conditions, see later discussions).
\subsection{Formulation 1}\label{sec:u_dep}
For the ease of discussion, we consider the linear system, which becomes
\begin{gather}
    \left\{\begin{array}{l}
        \mb{M}\ddot{\bu}+\mb{K}\bu+\sum\bbf_j=\mb{0}, \\
        \dot{\bbf}_j=-s_j\bbf_j+m_j\mb{K}\bu,
    \end{array}\right.
\end{gather}
with $\by_j=\mb{K}\bu$.
Assuming harmonic solutions for both $\bu$ and $\bbf_j$,
\begin{gather}\label{eq:harmonic_solution}
    \bu\left(t\right)=\mb{U}\exp\left(i\omega{}t\right),\qquad
    \bbf_j\left(t\right)=\mb{F}_j\exp\left(i\omega{}t\right),
\end{gather}
the second expression can be expressed in the frequency domain as
\begin{gather}
    i\omega\mb{F}_j=-s_j\mb{F}_j+m_j\mb{K}\mb{U},
\end{gather}
thus $\mb{F}_j=\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}\mb{K}\mb{U}$.
The characteristic equation can then be derived as
\begin{gather}\label{eq:disp_cha}
    \det\left(\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}\right)\mb{K}-\omega^2\mb{M}\right)=0.
\end{gather}
The dynamic stiffness $\mb{K}^*$ is
\begin{gather}
    \mb{K}^*=\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}\right)\mb{K}
\end{gather}
with storage stiffness $\mb{K}'$ and loss stiffness $\mb{K}''$
\begin{gather}\label{eq:u_stiffnesses}
    \mb{K}'=\left(1+\sum\dfrac{s_jm_j}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\right)\mb{K},\qquad
    \mb{K}''=-\sum\dfrac{m_j\omega}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\mb{K}
\end{gather}
such that $\mb{K}^*=\mb{K}'+\mb{K}''i$.
%The ratio $\eta_u$ between them is
%\begin{gather}
%\eta_u=-\dfrac{\sum\dfrac{m_j\omega}{s_j^2+\omega^2}}{1+\sum\dfrac{s_jm_j}{s_j^2+\omega^2}}.
%\end{gather}
One could identify
\begin{gather}
    \lim\limits_{\omega\rightarrow0}\mb{K}'=\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j}\right)\mb{K},\qquad
    \lim\limits_{\omega\rightarrow\infty}\mb{K}'=\mb{K}.
\end{gather}

Repeating the above procedure with $\by_j=\bu$, one can obtain the following stiffnesses:
\begin{gather}
    \mb{K}'=\mb{K}+\sum\dfrac{s_jm_j}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\mb{I},\qquad
    \mb{K}''=-\sum\dfrac{m_j\omega}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\mb{I}.
\end{gather}
By directly applying to displacement $\bu$, the loss stiffness $\mb{K}''$ no longer relies on the rate-independent stiffness $\mb{K}$.
The amount of energy dissipated thus does not change even if the system exhibits softening behaviour.
\subsection{Complex Realm Extension}
The above analyses assume $s_j$ and $m_j$ are real scalars.
One may notice that if $m_j$ and $s_j$ come in conjugate pairs, for example,
\begin{gather}
    m_j\in\mathbb{C},\qquad
    s_j\in\mathbb{C},\qquad
    m_{j+1}=\bar{m_j},\qquad
    s_{j+1}=\bar{s_j},
\end{gather}
then denoting $Z_j=\dfrac{s_jm_j}{s_j^2+\omega^2}$, one would have
\begin{gather}
    Z_j+Z_{j+1}=Z_j+\bar{Z_j}=2\Re\left(Z_j\right)
\end{gather}
to be strictly real--valued.
This simply implies that it is possible to extend the definition of parameters $s_j$ and $m_j$ to the complex realm, providing that they come in conjugate pairs.
In the following, it is thus assumed $s_j\in\mathbb{C}$ and $m_j\in\mathbb{C}$ with the additional constraint implied.
\subsection{Formulation 2}\label{sec:a_dep}
Alternatively, one can pick $\by_j=\mb{M}\ba$.
The system is
\begin{gather}
    \left\{\begin{array}{l}
        \mb{M}\ddot{\bu}+\mb{K}\bu+\sum\bbf_j=\mb{0}, \\
        \dot{\bbf}_j=-s_j\bbf_j+m_j\mb{M}\ba.
    \end{array}\right.
\end{gather}
Adopting a similar approach, the characteristic equation can be derived as
\begin{gather}\label{eq:acc_cha}
    \det\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}}\mb{K}-\omega^2\mb{M}\right)=0.
\end{gather}
The loss ratio stays the same since the scalar coefficient is the reciprocal of that in the previous case.

It is possible to show that \eqsref{eq:acc_cha} is in fact identical to \eqsref{eq:disp_cha}.
Rewriting the scalar coefficient into the matrix form with $\mb{s}=\begin{bmatrix}
        s_1 & s_2 & \cdots & s_j
    \end{bmatrix}^\mT$ and $\mb{m}=\begin{bmatrix}
        m_1 & m_2 & \cdots & m_j
    \end{bmatrix}^\mT$ and applying the Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury formula, one could obtain
\begin{gather}
    \begin{split}
        \dfrac{1}{1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}} & =\left(1+\mb{1}^\mT\diag{\mb{s}+i\omega}^{-1}\mb{m}\right)^{-1} \\&=1-\mb{1}^\mT\left(\diag{\mb{s}+i\omega}+\mb{m}\mb{1}^\mT\right)^{-1}\mb{m}.
    \end{split}
\end{gather}
Denoting the eigenvalues of matrix $\diag{\mb{s}}+\mb{m}\mb{1}^\mT$ as $s'_j$ and the corresponding left and right eigenvectors as $\mb{\phi}^l_j$ and $\mb{\phi}^r_j$, then
\begin{gather}
    \dfrac{1}{1+\sum\dfrac{m_j}{s_j+i\omega}}=1+\sum\dfrac{m'_j}{s_j'+i\omega}\qquad\text{with}\quad{}m'_j=-\dfrac{\left\langle\mb{1},\mb{\phi}^r_j\right\rangle\left\langle\mb{\phi}^l_j,\mb{m}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\mb{\phi}^r_j,\mb{\phi}^l_j\right\rangle}.
\end{gather}

For any given sets of $s_j$ and $m_j$, it is thus possible to find the corresponding sets $s'_j$ and $m'_j$ to convert between \eqsref{eq:acc_cha} and \eqsref{eq:disp_cha}.
This process naturally extends the definition of $s_j$ and $m_j$ to the complex realm.
Even if the input pairs are real, for example, $s_j,m_j\in\mathbb{R}$, the corresponding $s'_j$ and $m'_j$ can be complex, simply due to the fact that $s'_j$, which are eigenvalues of matrix $\diag{\mb{s}}+\mb{m}\mb{1}^\mT$, are not necessarily real.
In absence of other energy dissipating actions, the two formulations are thus equivalent.
This equivalence may offer numerical advantages when the mass matrix is constant, as is often the case, and more sparse than the stiffness matrix, particularly under a lumped mass formulation.
\subsection{Formulation 3}\label{sec:v_dep_k}
We consider $\by_j=\mb{K}\bv$, the system becomes
\begin{gather}\label{eq:vel_formulation}
    \left\{\begin{array}{l}
        \mb{M}\ddot{\bu}+\mb{K}\bu+\sum\bbf_j=\mb{0}, \\
        \dot{\bbf}_j=-s_j\bbf_j+m_j\mb{K}\dot{\bu}.
    \end{array}\right.
\end{gather}
The characteristic equation can be derived as
\begin{gather}\label{eq:vel_cha}
    \det\left(\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_ji\omega}{s_j+i\omega}\right)\mb{K}-\omega^2\mb{M}\right)=0.
\end{gather}
Thus,
\begin{gather}
    \mb{K}'=\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_j\omega^2}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\right)\mb{K},\qquad
    \mb{K}''=\sum\dfrac{s_jm_j\omega}{s_j^2+\omega^2}\mb{K}.
\end{gather}
One could identify
\begin{gather}
    \lim\limits_{\omega\rightarrow0}\mb{K}'=\mb{K},\qquad
    \lim\limits_{\omega\rightarrow\infty}\mb{K}'=\left(1+\sum{}m_j\right)\mb{K}.
\end{gather}
A careful comparison reveals that \eqsref{eq:vel_formulation} is in fact equivalent to the model by \citet{Huang2019}.
\subsection{Formulation 4}\label{sec:v_dep_m}
Alternatively, consider $\by_j=\mb{M}\dot{\ba}$, the corresponding characteristic equation can be expressed as
\begin{gather}\label{eq:vel_cha2}
    \det\left(\mb{K}-\left(1+\sum\dfrac{m_ji\omega}{s_j+i\omega}\right)\omega^2\mb{M}\right)=0,
\end{gather}
resulting the dynamic stiffness
\begin{gather}
    \mb{K}^*=\dfrac{1}{1+\sum\dfrac{m_ji\omega}{s_j+i\omega}}\mb{K}.
\end{gather}
Similar to the previous derivation, via partial fraction decomposition, it is possible to find $s'_j$ and $m'_j$ (explicit expressions are not shown for brevity) such that
\begin{gather}
    \dfrac{1}{1+\sum\dfrac{m_ji\omega}{s_j+i\omega}}=1+\sum\dfrac{m'_ji\omega}{s'_j+i\omega},
\end{gather}
effectively making \eqsref{eq:vel_cha2} an equivalent alternative formulation of \eqsref{eq:vel_cha}.
Because computing and storing $\dot{\ba}=\dddot{\bu}$ is \textbf{not} supported in typical analysis frameworks, this practically restrictive formulation is presented solely to establish the equivalence between the two forms.
Should $\dddot{\bu}$ be tracked, the system can leverage the unique characteristics of the mass matrix detailed in previous sections.
\subsection{Remarks}
Borrowing the relation discussed in the work by \citet{Chang2024}, the above proposed formulations can also be equivalently expressed as
\begin{gather}
    \br+\sum{}g_j*\by_j=\mb{p}
\end{gather}
where $g_j*\by_j$ stands for the convolution between the kernel $g_j$ and $\by_j$, that is,
\begin{gather}\label{eq:conv}
    \left(g_j*\by_j\right)\left(t\right)=\int_0^tg_j\left(t-\tau\right)\by_j\left(\tau\right)\md{\tau},
\end{gather}
with $g_j=g_j\left(t\right)=m_j\exp\left(-s_jt\right)$ and $\by_j$ possessing various forms.
This type of equivalence provides an alternative perspective to understand various damping formulations.
The damping characteristics, parameterised by $s_j$ and $m_j$, may be interpreted as modifications to the kernel functions $g_j$, enabling the desired damping response to be realised through kernel design.
\section{Numerical Implementation}
\subsection{A Reference Algorithm}
The algorithm discussed in the previous work \citep{Chang2024} is repurposed here to cater various types of systems.
The governing system is
\begin{gather}\label{eq:eqv_sys}
    \left\{
    \begin{array}{l}
        \dot{\bbf}_j=-s_j\bbf_j+m_j\by_j, \\
        \br+\sum\bbf_j=\mb{p}.
    \end{array}\right.
\end{gather}
The first expression is a first-order ODE (of $\bbf_j$) that can be integrated by the (implicit) trapezoidal rule, which is second-order accurate and A-stable,
\begin{gather}
    \bbf_{j,n+1}=\bbf_{j,n}+\dfrac{\Delta{}t}{2}\left(\dot{\bbf}_{j,n}+\dot{\bbf}_{j,n+1}\right).
\end{gather}
Expanding gives
\begin{gather}
    \bbf_{j,n+1}=\bbf_{j,n}+\dfrac{\Delta{}t}{2}\left(\left(-s_j\bbf_{j,n}+m_j\by_{j,n}\right)+\left(-s_j\bbf_{j,n+1}+m_j\by_{j,n+1}\right)\right),
\end{gather}
one may further rearrange to obtain
\begin{gather}\label{eq:discretised_c}
    \bbf_{j,n+1}=\dfrac{2-s_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\bbf_{j,n}+\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j,n}+\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j,n+1},
\end{gather}
in which subscripts $\left(\cdot\right)_n$ and $\left(\cdot\right)_{n+1}$ denote the corresponding quantity at $t_n$ and $t_{n+1}=t_n+\Delta{}t$.

Now assuming the equation of motion is satisfied at $t_{n+1}$, which is valid for a wide range of time integration methods (including the well known Newmark method), the second expression is
\begin{gather}\label{eq:residual}
    \br_{n+1}+\sum\dfrac{2-s_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\bbf_{j,n}+\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j,n}+\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j,n+1}=\mb{p}_{n+1}.
\end{gather}
Differentiation leads to the following revised effective stiffness $\bhat{K}_{n+1}$,
\begin{gather}\label{eq:revised_k}
    \bhat{K}_{n+1}=\bbar{K}_{n+1}+\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\ddfrac{\by_{j,n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}.
\end{gather}
in which the conventional effective stiffness $\bbar{K}_{n+1}$ is
\begin{gather}\label{eq:effective_k}
    \bbar{K}_{n+1}=\ddfrac{\br_{n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}=\mb{K}_{n+1}+\mb{C}_{n+1}\ddfrac{\bv_{n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}+\mb{M}_{n+1}\ddfrac{\ba_{n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}.
\end{gather}
By the above, we have assumed that the contributions of $\bu$, $\bv=\dot{\bu}$ and $\ba=\ddot{\bu}$ are separable, indicating no coupling among them, then the resistance $\br$ can be split into three components
\begin{gather}
    \br=\br_{\bu}+\br_{\bv}+\br_{\ba},
\end{gather}
such that $\br_{\baleph}=\br_{\baleph}\left(\baleph\right)$ are functions of $\baleph$ where $\baleph\in\left\{\bu,\bv,\ba\right\}$.

It thus can be concluded from \eqsref{eq:revised_k} that to account for this additional energy dissipating term $\bbf$, only an additional matrix $\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\ddfrac{\by_{j,n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}$ needs to be added to the original effective stiffness $\bbar{K}_{n+1}$.
In some certain cases, this additional matrix can be very efficiently formulated.
For example, if $\by_j\in\{\br_{\bu},\br_{\ba}\}$, then $\ddfrac{\by_{j,n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}$ is simply the stiffness matrix $\mb{K}_{n+1}$ (or the mass matrix $\mb{M}_{n+1}$) that is already available in the original formulation.
In some other special cases, this matrix can be diagonal, for example, with $\by_j\in\{\bu,\bv,\ba\}$, then $\ddfrac{\by_{j,n+1}}{\bu_{n+1}}=\alpha\mb{I}$ is a diagonal matrix with possibly a non-unit scalar $\alpha$.
Adding such an diagonal matrix to $\bhat{K}_{n+1}$ is almost computationally trivial.

\algoref{algo:general_algo} presents an algorithm that integrates the proposed damping formulation into existing iterative analysis workflow.
Even though $\bbf_j$ can be complex due to complex $s_j$ and $m_j$, as long as conjugate pairs of $s_j$ and $m_j$ are used, $\bbf=\sum\bbf_j$ is real--valued.
The same also applies to $\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}$ thus $\bhat{K}$ is real--valued.
\begin{breakablealgorithm}
    \setstretch{2}
    \caption{iteration body of solving dynamic systems with general damping}\label{algo:general_algo}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \State \textbf{*Schema: Newmark + Trapezoidal}
        \State \textbf{Input}: $\bbar{K}$, $\bu$, $\bv$, $\ba$, $\br$, $\bp$ (quantities obtained via conventional manner as if there is no general damping); damping history/parameters $\bbf_{j,n}$, $\by_{j,n}$, $m_j$, $s_j$
        \State \textbf{Output}: $\bu$
        \State \faMicrochip~compute energy dissipating force $\bbf_{j}=\dfrac{2-s_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\bbf_{j,n}+\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j,n}+\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\by_{j}$\Comment{\eqsref{eq:discretised_c}}
        \State \faMicrochip~compute revised effective stiffness $\bhat{K}=\bbar{K}+\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}\ddfrac{\by_{j}}{\bu}$\Comment{\eqsref{eq:revised_k}}
        \State \faMicrochip~compute revised resistance $\bhat{r}=\br+\sum\bbf_j$
        \State $\delta\bu=\bhat{K}^{-1}\left(\mb{p}-\bhat{r}\right)$
        \State update and return $\bu\leftarrow\bu+\delta\bu$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

The proposed damping formulations can be applied at both the global model and element levels.
Consequently, the energy dissipating force $\bbf$ can be flexibly formulated using either node-based or element-based representations.

One shall notice that the proposed formulations has the following advantages.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It retains the size of the original problem/system.
    \item The parameters $s_j$ and $m_j$ accept complex values, providing extra flexibility customising the desired damping response.
    \item State determination of additional damping history $\bbf_j$ is constant and only involves matrix--vector multiplications.
    \item The consistent tangent stiffness that allows quadratic convergence rate can be trivially assembled, for example, using different scalar weights in weighted summation if $\by_j\in\left\{\br_{\bu},\br_{\bv},\br_{\ba}\right\}$ or assembling additional diagonal matrices if $\by_j\in\left\{\bu,\bv,\ba\right\}$.
          In problems where the mass matrix $\mb{M}$ is constant and/or lumped, the extra computational efforts required would be minimized.
    \item The reciprocal formulations are equivalent mathematically.
          In absence of other energy dissipating actions (such as conventional viscous damping), one form can be converted into another.
          Instead of scaling $\mb{K}$, it is possible to scale $\mb{M}$ in the equivalent form.
          Noting that in global time integration methods that express the equation of motion in terms of $\bu$, $\mb{M}$ needs to be scaled anyway by a factor, for example, $\dfrac{1}{\beta\Delta{}t^2}$ in Newmark method, accounting for the proposed damping formulation only needs to revise this scalar factor to $\dfrac{1}{\beta\Delta{}t^2}\sum\dfrac{m_j\Delta{}t}{2+s_j\Delta{}t}$.
\end{enumerate}

The preceding analyses assume no external damping actions.
Consequently, the derived loss stiffnesses and loss ratios hold strictly in the absence of additional velocity dependent energy dissipation mechanisms, such as conventional viscous damping.
Nonetheless, a non-trivial $\mb{C}$ is maintained in \eqsref{eq:effective_k} to preserve structural generality.
