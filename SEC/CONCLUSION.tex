\section{Conclusions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The general damping formulation \eqsref{eq:general_eom} is analysed via four distinct variants: \secref{sec:u_dep} with \secref{sec:fix}, \secref{sec:a_dep} with \secref{sec:fix}, \secref{sec:v_dep_k}, and \secref{sec:v_dep_m}.
\item Pairwise equivalences exist between \secref{sec:u_dep} and \secref{sec:a_dep}, as well as between \secref{sec:v_dep_k} and \secref{sec:v_dep_m}.
All four formulations accommodate complex conjugate parameter pairs ($m_j$, $s_j$), offering enhanced flexibility for tailoring damping responses.
All four formulations are mathematically identical in the frequency domain, yielding the same storage and loss stiffnesses, and thus the same dynamic response.
\item \eqsref{eq:general_eom} with \eqsref{eq:linear_de} can be recast into the convolution form of \eqsref{eq:conv}, providing an alternative interpretation.
\item The formulation in \secref{sec:v_dep_k} extends the model proposed by \citet{Huang2019}.
\end{itemize}

The presented nonviscous damping formulations are implemented in the open-source finite element analysis framework \texttt{suanPan} \citep{Chang2026}.
All numerical models and the relevant scripts are available in this repository\footnote{https://github.com/TLCFEM/universal-damping}.
