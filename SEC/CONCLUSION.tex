\section{Conclusions}
The following findings can be drawn.
\begin{itemize}
\item The general damping formulation \eqsref{eq:general_eom} is analysed through several distinct variants.
\item Three specific formulations are practically useful: \eqsref{eq:u_final}, \eqsref{eq:v_final} and \eqsref{eq:a_final}.
These formulations are mathematically identical in the frequency domain, yielding the same storage and loss stiffnesses, and thus the same dynamic response.
\item Pairwise equivalences exist between \eqsref{eq:u_final} and \eqsref{eq:a_final}, as well as between \eqsref{eq:v_final} and a revised version of \secref{sec:v_dep_m} (not shown).
All formulations accommodate complex conjugate parameter pairs ($m_j$, $s_j$), offering enhanced flexibility for tailoring damping responses.
\item \eqsref{eq:general_eom} with \eqsref{eq:linear_de} can be recast into the convolution form of \eqsref{eq:conv}, providing an alternative interpretation.
\item The formulation \eqsref{eq:v_final} extends the model proposed by \citet{Huang2019}.
It is, however, computationally expensive.
The equivalent formulations \eqsref{eq:u_final} and \eqsref{eq:a_final} are less expensive computationally.
\end{itemize}

The presented nonviscous damping formulations are implemented in the open-source finite element analysis framework \texttt{suanPan} \citep{Chang2026}.
All numerical examples and the relevant scripts are available in this repository\footnote{https://github.com/TLCFEM/universal-damping}.
